
**Money**: A commodity accepted by general consent as a medium of economic exchange. It is the medium in which prices and values are expressed; as currency it circulates anonymously from person to person and country , thus facilitating trade and it is the principal measure of wealth.

**Currency**: A system of money in general use within a country or economic region, acting as a medium of exchange for goods and services. It includes physical forms like banknotes and coins as well as digital money and is issued by governments or central banks to facilitate trade. 

**Asset**: Anything that has economic value and can generate future benefit.
* Examples: cash, stocks, bonds, property, loans receivable
* Things you own

**Liability**: Something you owe (loans, credit card debt, mortgages).

**Equity**: Residual ownership after liabilities are paid.
* Formula: Equity = Assets - Liabilities
* Represents shareholder ownership in a company
* In banking systems: often split into share capital + retained earnings

**Revenue**: Total income generated from business operations before costs.
* For banks: interest income + fees
* For SaaS: subscription income
* Important: revenue is not equal to profit

**Profit** (Net Income)
* Includes: operating costs, interest taxes
* Key metric for valuation (P/E ratios, etc.)
* Often called "bottom line"

**Cash Flow**: Actual movement of money in and out of a business.
* Types:
	* Operating cash flow (core business)
	* Investing cash flow (assets, acquisitions)
	* Financing cash flows (loans, equity issuance)
* Important: a company can be profitable but have bad cash flow

**Balance Sheet**: A snapshot at a specific time.
* Shows:
	* Assets
	* Liabilites
	* Equity
* What the company owns and owes right now

**Income Statement**: Performance over a time period (monthly/quarterly/yearly)
* Shows Revenue, Expenses, Profit
* How the company performed

**Interest Rate**: Cost of borrowing money or return on lending money.
* Expressed as % per year
* Central banks (like BoE) set base rates
* Drives mortgages, loans and asset pricing

**Compound Interest**: Interest earned on both original money and accumulated interest.
* Formula growth is exponential over time
* Key concept in investing and debt accumulation
* "Snowball effect" of money

**Principal**: The original amount of money invested or borrowed.
* Example: £10,000 loan, the principle is £10,000
* Interest is calculated on this base (or growing base in compounding)

**Diversification**: Spreading risk across multiple assets.
* Not relying on one asset class
* Example: stocks, bonds and real estate.
* Reduces Idiosyncratic risk (single-asset failure).

**Volatility**: How much price moves up and down over time.
* High volatility = unstable prices (crypto, tech stocks)
* Low volatility = stable assets (government bonds)
* Often measured using standard deviation

**Liquidity**: How quickly and easily an asset can be converted into cash.
* High liquidity: cash, major stocks
* Low liquidity: property, private equity.
* Important for risk management in banking

**Bond**: A loan you give to an issuer (government/company).
* You get regular interest (coupon) and principal at maturity.
* Bonds are priced based on interest rates and credit risk.

**Stock** (Equity Share): A unit of ownership in a company.
* Gives voting rights (sometimes), dividends (sometimes) and capital gains.
* Fluctuates based on market expectations

**Market Capitalisation**: total value of a companies equity.
* Share price * total shares
* Used to classify companies:
	* Small - cap
	* Mid - cap
	* Large - cap
* Reflects market perception of value

**Dividend**: Cash payment from company profits to shareholders.
* Not all companies pay dividends
* Often quarterly 
* Mature companies (banks, utilities) tend to pay more.

**Return on Investment** (ROI): Measures profitability of an investment.
* Formula: ROI = (Gain - Cost)/Cost
* Used everywhere: project, portfolios, business cases
* Doesn't consider time unless adjusted (annualised ROI).

**Leverage**: Using borrowed money to amplify returns (and risk)
* Example: £1 equity + £9 debt to invest £10
* If asset rises then higher gains
* If asset falls then amplified losses
* Core concept in banking risk models
